01 · The challenge

Big cities can feel surprisingly small.

Finding an event is easy. Finding something that feels welcoming, fits your schedule, and helps you meet your people is the harder part.

01

The problem

Local activities are scattered across social feeds, ticketing platforms, group chats, and community boards. People spend too much time searching and still feel unsure about attending alone.

  • Event details are inconsistent or incomplete.
  • Distance, cost, and availability are hard to compare.
  • First-time attendees worry about showing up solo.
↗

The design opportunity

Make local discovery feel personal, low-pressure, and actionable—from the first search to the moment someone walks through the door.

  • Personalized discovery with useful filters.
  • Clear event details and transparent costs.
  • Save, RSVP, and manage plans in one place.

08 · Handoff & delivery

Designed to move from prototype to product.

Developer-ready implementation notes, content guidance, and accessibility considerations for the next phase.

↗

Interaction specs

Document loading, empty, selected, saved, and confirmation states. Keep filters, RSVP actions, and navigation predictable.

▧

Assets & tokens

Use the included color values, type scale, spacing, radii, reusable cards, and consistent semantic status styles.

◎

Accessibility checks

Maintain keyboard focus, clear labels, sufficient contrast, semantic controls, and readable tap targets at mobile widths.

Validation plan

What should be tested next?

  • Run moderated usability sessions with people new to a neighborhood and frequent event-goers.
  • Measure time to find a suitable event, RSVP completion, and confidence about attending alone.
  • Test screen-reader behavior, keyboard-only navigation, mobile layout, and error recovery.
  • Replace illustrative personas and assumptions with observed research and measured results before presenting this as validated product research.
